# Emergency essentials — print this one page

**Print this now, while you have power, and keep it with the drive.** When the battery is
dead, paper is all that works. These are **short reminders** drawn from the manuals on this
drive — not the full instructions. While you still can, open and read the sources cited at
the bottom; practice the steps before you need them.

**Get professional help first if it is reachable** — call your local emergency number
(911 in the US) or call for help by radio. Use this page only when no help is available.

---

## Severe bleeding

1. Press **hard and directly on the wound** with a cloth or your hand. Do not let up to peek.
2. Keep pressing. Add more cloth on top; do not remove soaked cloth. Raise the limb if you can.
3. If a **limb** is bleeding so badly it may kill them and pressure is not stopping it, put a
   **tourniquet** 5–8 cm (2–3 in) above the wound, between it and the heart — not on a joint.
   Tighten until the bleeding stops. **Write down the time.** Do not loosen it; get help.

## Not breathing — CPR (adult, hands-only)

1. Check response and breathing. If unresponsive and not breathing normally, send for help.
2. Put the heel of one hand on the **centre of the chest**, the other hand on top.
3. Push **hard and fast**, about **5–6 cm (2 in)** deep, **100–120 pushes a minute**. Let the
   chest rise fully between pushes.
4. Do not stop until they wake and breathe, someone trained takes over, or you cannot continue.

## Choking

- If they can cough, let them cough. If they **cannot** breathe, cough or speak: give up to
  **5 firm back blows** between the shoulder blades, then **5 abdominal thrusts** (inward and
  up, above the navel). Repeat until it clears or they go unconscious — then start CPR.

## Make water safe

- When in doubt, **boil it**: a **rolling boil for 1 minute** (**3 minutes above ~2,000 m /
  6,500 ft**) kills germs. Let cloudy water settle and pour off the clear water first.
- No way to boil? Use the filtering and chemical-treatment steps in the Army survival manual (`Manuals/ArmyFieldManuals/`).

## Signal for help

- **Three** of anything means "help": three whistle blasts, three fires, three shouts; repeat.
- Make yourself **big and bright**: ground signals in a clearing, a mirror/flash toward
  aircraft, a fire by night and smoke by day.
- **If lost, usually stay put** — you are easier to find. Keep warm and dry; keep signalling.

## Call for help by radio — key frequencies

| Use | Frequency / channel |
|---|---|
| NOAA weather (US, listen) | 162.400–162.550 MHz |
| Marine VHF distress & calling | **Channel 16 (156.800 MHz)** |
| Aviation emergency (voice) | **121.500 MHz** |
| Distress beacon (EPIRB/ELT/PLB) | **406 MHz** (satellite alert, not voice) |
| FRS/GMRS walkie-talkies | no guaranteed emergency watch — agree a channel in advance |

Say **who you are, where you are, and what is wrong**, then listen. A calling frequency is not
a promise someone is listening. Transmit only as the law allows (emergencies are the exception).
