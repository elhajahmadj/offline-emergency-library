# Medical — Offline Health & First-Aid Reference

Offline medical reference for emergencies. **Two kinds of files:**

- **PDFs** → open in **Edge or any web browser**. **No software needed.**
- **`.zim` archives** → open with **Kiwix** (portable, in `E:\Kiwix-Reader\`). See
  `E:\Kiwix-Reader\HOW-TO-USE-KIWIX`.

> **About `Natural-Medicine\`:** these are **historical documents** (1600s–1900s), kept for
> reference and interest. Much of their content is outdated or simply wrong by modern science,

> care use WikiMed, *Where There Is No Doctor*, and qualified help whenever possible.

## MedlinePlus health topics (opt-in)

If the drive was made with `-MedlinePlus` (Windows) or `--medlineplus` (macOS/Linux), open
[MedlinePlus/MedlinePlus-Health-Topics.html](MedlinePlus/MedlinePlus-Health-Topics.html) in
any web browser. It is one page of plain-language health topics in English and
the same in Spanish: what an illness, symptom or test is, and what to do about it. Type in the
search box (a symptom, illness, test or body part), or switch language with the buttons.

This is **consumer reference, not clinical advice**: the summaries are written for the public
and do not cover doses, procedures or emergency treatment. For first aid use the books above.

**What is not included:** MedlinePlus's Medical Encyclopedia (A.D.A.M.) and its drug and
supplement pages (American Society of Health-System Pharmacists) are copyrighted and licensed
to NLM only, so they are not on this drive. Links to them in a summary need internet.

**Source and license:** the health topic summaries come from the official MedlinePlus XML
file published by the US National Library of Medicine (https://medlineplus.gov/xml.html),
downloaded when the drive was made (the page shows the date). NLM states that health topic
summaries are not copyrighted (public domain) and asks for this credit: *Source: MedlinePlus,
National Library of Medicine* (https://medlineplus.gov/about/using/usingcontent/). Images
are not included.

## Childbirth and maternal health

**Seek skilled medical help and arrange transport whenever possible.** These WHO
references support trained health workers; they do not make unassisted childbirth safe
or replace hands-on training, medicines, equipment or emergency care.

For an emergency delivery when no skilled help is reachable, the plain-language
[Emergency Childbirth](Emergency-Childbirth/Emergency%20Childbirth%20%28US%20Civil%20Defense%29.html)
guide (US Office of Civil Defense / Public Health Service, public domain) is written for anyone,
not just clinicians. It opens in any browser. The WHO references below are for trained workers.

Open these PDFs directly in a browser, without Kiwix or AI:

- [Managing complications in pregnancy and childbirth (second edition, 2017)](Manuals-PDF/WHO%20Managing%20Complications%20in%20Pregnancy%20and%20Childbirth%202017.pdf):
  a guide for midwives and doctors providing district-hospital care. Use its contents,
  bookmarks or PDF search to locate pregnancy, childbirth, postpartum and newborn topics.
- [WHO recommendations on maternal health (second edition, 2025)](Manuals-PDF/WHO%20Recommendations%20on%20Maternal%20Health%202025.pdf):
  a compilation of WHO's approved maternal-health recommendations (antenatal, labour and
  birth, postnatal, complications) with the evidence behind them; written for clinicians and
  programme planners, not a step-by-step birth manual.

**Edition caution:** WHO flags that several recommendations in the 2017 manual have
subsequently been updated. The 2025 compilation is also a dated snapshot, not a live
guideline service. Check current WHO and local guidance with qualified clinicians before
going offline; do not assume either PDF is the latest clinical guidance.

## ⚠️ Important

This is **reference information, not a substitute for a doctor.** Models and books can be
wrong or out of date. In a real emergency, get professional medical help whenever
possible. Use this when no help is available, and double-check anything critical.
