MedlinePlus health topics (opt-in)

If the drive was made with -MedlinePlus (Windows) or --medlineplus (macOS/Linux), open MedlinePlus/MedlinePlus-Health-Topics.html in any web browser. It is one page of plain-language health topics in English and the same in Spanish: what an illness, symptom or test is, and what to do about it. Type in the search box (a symptom, illness, test or body part), or switch language with the buttons.

This is consumer reference, not clinical advice: the summaries are written for the public and do not cover doses, procedures or emergency treatment. For first aid use the books above.

What is not included: MedlinePlus's Medical Encyclopedia (A.D.A.M.) and its drug and supplement pages (American Society of Health-System Pharmacists) are copyrighted and licensed to NLM only, so they are not on this drive. Links to them in a summary need internet.

Source and license: the health topic summaries come from the official MedlinePlus XML file published by the US National Library of Medicine (https://medlineplus.gov/xml.html), downloaded when the drive was made (the page shows the date). NLM states that health topic summaries are not copyrighted (public domain) and asks for this credit: Source: MedlinePlus, National Library of Medicine (https://medlineplus.gov/about/using/usingcontent/). Images are not included.

Childbirth and maternal health

Seek skilled medical help and arrange transport whenever possible. These WHO references support trained health workers; they do not make unassisted childbirth safe or replace hands-on training, medicines, equipment or emergency care.

Open these PDFs directly in a browser, without Kiwix or AI:

  • Managing complications in pregnancy and childbirth (second edition, 2017): a guide for midwives and doctors providing district-hospital care. Use its contents, bookmarks or PDF search to locate pregnancy, childbirth, postpartum and newborn topics.
  • WHO recommendations on maternal health (second edition, 2025): a compilation of WHO's approved maternal-health recommendations (antenatal, labour and birth, postnatal, complications) with the evidence behind them; written for clinicians and programme planners, not a step-by-step birth manual.

Edition caution: WHO flags that several recommendations in the 2017 manual have subsequently been updated. The 2025 compilation is also a dated snapshot, not a live guideline service. Check current WHO and local guidance with qualified clinicians before going offline; do not assume either PDF is the latest clinical guidance.
