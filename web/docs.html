Getting started

Open the drive and look for START-HERE at the top level — double-click START-HERE.html (it opens in any browser, no internet needed). It’s the front door: a short map of everything on the drive and where to go for each need.

Next to it you’ll find CHECK-DRIVE (confirms the files copied intact) and the I-NEED-TO quick index below. Nothing here “installs” onto your computer — the whole library runs straight from the drive.

Offline AI assistant

A chat assistant that runs entirely on your own computer — no internet, no account. Good for “explain this”, “what do I do if…”, and summarising the manuals in plain language.

  1. Launch it from the AI folder on the drive (Run-Offline-AI / the chat launcher for your system). First start loads the model into memory and can take a minute.
  2. Pick the model for the job. A small model always ships for speed; larger chat models (and an optional translator) are there if you selected them — bigger is slower but more capable. No GPU is required; it runs on an ordinary CPU.
  3. Ask your own documents. The separate document-Q&A app (GPT4All) can read the bundled PDFs and cite them — point it at the manuals folder and ask. See Ask Your Documents.

Kiwix archives (Wikipedia, books, courses)

Kiwix is the reader for the big .zim archives — offline Wikipedia, medical Wikipedia, iFixit repair guides, Appropedia DIY, and (if you chose them) Wikivoyage, Wikibooks and Khan Academy.

  1. Open the reader from the maps/archives area of the drive (Kiwix desktop app for your system), or install Kiwix on a phone.
  2. Open a .zim file from the archives folder — each archive is one .zim. Kiwix lists them once the folder is added.
  3. Search within an archive from the box at the top; everything works with no connection.

Offline maps

Two ways to use maps, both offline after the one-time download:

On a computer — no phone needed

Run the bundled desktop map viewer (MapViewer-Desktop/VIEW-MAPS-OFFLINE). It draws your region’s OsmAnd maps on Windows, macOS and Linux. First use unzips the maps. US street detail is also available as browser maps if you selected them.

On a phone

Android: sideload the bundled OsmAnd~ app (no internet needed). iPhone/iPad: install OsmAnd from the App Store while you still have a connection, then import your region’s map files from the drive.

Manuals & PDFs

The field manuals, medical references, survival, food-growing and metalworking PDFs live under Manuals/ and Medical/. Open any of them in a normal PDF reader. The Manuals-READ-ME-FIRST guide explains the focused core set and which book covers what.

Licences vary — most are public-domain US-Government works or out-of-copyright books; a few medical titles are non-commercial, and two Army manuals carry distribution/copyright caveats. The full breakdown is on the Sources page.

Troubleshooting & FAQ

A file won’t open

PDFs open in any PDF reader or browser; .html guides open in any browser; .zim archives need the Kiwix reader (not a browser). If a launcher doesn’t start, open its folder and run the version for your operating system.

Do I need internet to use any of this?

No. After the one-time download, everything on the drive works with no connection. The only things that need a connection are first-time app installs on a phone (OsmAnd/Kiwix from an app store) — do those before you go off-grid.

The AI is slow or runs out of memory

Use a smaller chat model, and close other programs. Larger models need more RAM and are slower on a CPU. The document-Q&A app is lighter if you mainly want to search the manuals.

How much space does everything take?

It depends on what you picked — a full build with maps and Wikipedia is large. The Picker shows a running size total as you select, and the Sources page lists every component.

Is this website the whole library?

No — this site is browse-only (the picker, docs, sources and about). The actual library is the drive you build with the installer. Downloading only makes sense locally.

Where do I get help?

Open an issue on the project’s GitHub.
