Radio Frequency Reference

US baseline - checked 2026-11-03. All frequencies below are in MHz. Save this file with its HTML companion; open the HTML in a browser and use Print / Save as PDF. All tables and notes are included offline. Source links are for checking updates while online. See the Radio Guide (Markdown) for equipment and calling procedures.

Before transmitting: follow the rules for your country, radio service, license, equipment and location. Receiving public broadcasts generally needs no transmit license; local scanner, interception and disclosure restrictions may still apply. An amateur license does not authorize marine, aviation or GMRS transmissions. Emergency exceptions are service-specific, not blanket permission to transmit anywhere. FCC 97.403/97.405 concern amateur stations. [1]

Region matters: these personal-radio channel numbers and amateur privileges are US FCC references. They are not a worldwide allocation table. Marine radio US/International/Canada settings can change channel behavior. Check the national regulator and local channel/band plan before traveling or programming transmit memories. A calling frequency is not a promise that anyone is listening.

Marine VHF - select the correct country channel group

Use a marine radio for marine service, not for casual land communications. The following is a selected US operational reference, not the complete international marine channel plan. Voice entries use FM simplex: ship transmit and receive frequencies are the same. Channel 70 is digital, not voice. [5]

ChannelFrequencyUse
16156.800International distress, urgency, safety and initial calling; move working traffic when directed

22A / 1022157.100US Coast Guard liaison/working channel after initial contact or instructions

22A / 1022 is the simplex ship-frequency channel, not international duplex channel 22. ITU's four-digit convention prefixes "10" to identify use of the ship frequency alone; older US radios commonly show the "A" suffix. [5, 6]

Local Vessel Traffic Service and bridge-to-bridge rules can override a generic list. In specified lower Mississippi waterways, covered vessels use 67, 156.375 for bridge-to-bridge service rather than 13; consult the exact geographic limits in 33 CFR 26.03(e). Do not improvise a DSC distress test: learn the radio's test procedure and program its correct MMSI beforehand. [5]

406 MHz bandApproved EPIRB / ELT / PLB; digital beaconSatellite distress alert, not a voice calling channel
121.5 beacon signalBeacon homing transmitterLocal search-team homing; separate from 406 MHz satellite alerting

EPIRB = maritime beacon; ELT = aircraft beacon; PLB = personal locator beacon. Register a 406 MHz beacon, keep the registration current, and use its approved self-test procedure. Activate for genuine distress according to its instructions; do not try to send beacon messages using a programmable voice radio. [7, 8]

Cospas-Sarsat stopped satellite detection of 121.5/243 MHz analog beacon alerts in 2009. This did not discontinue aviation emergency voice on those frequencies, or local homing. A scanner hearing 121.5 is not a satellite alert receiver. Marine rules prohibit voice telephony on 243 MHz. [5, 7, 8]

Amateur radio - calling frequencies and band-plan summary

US stations / ITU Region 2 baseline. A license, permitted mode and suitable equipment are required. ARRL/IARU band plans guide coordination; FCC rules set the legal limits. Local repeater coordinators and emergency nets may use different channels. Listen first; give priority to existing emergency traffic. There is no universal amateur distress channel or guaranteed rescue watch.

Technicians have voice privileges on the listed VHF/UHF bands, subject to the same mode and local restrictions. Their HF voice privilege is 28.300-28.500 SSB, 200 W PEP maximum; it does not include 29.600 FM or the HF emergency activity centers above. Extra/Advanced privileges differ from the General segments. 60 m has special channel/segment, power and emission rules that changed in 2026; consult current 97.303(h), 97.305 and 97.313 rather than an old five-channel card. The separate 219-220 MHz amateur allocation is data-only, not included as a voice band here. [9]

Before relying on a repeater, record its output, input/offset, tone, owner permission and fallback simplex channel. Do not assume every 2 m or 70 cm repeater uses the same offset or that it has backup power.
