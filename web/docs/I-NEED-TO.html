🆘 I need to… — emergency quick index

If professional help is reachable, get it first. Call your local emergency number (911 in the US), or use the radio guide to call for help. Use this drive when no professional help is available.

This page sends you straight to the right file. It is not the instructions — it points to the manual that has them. The manuals can be wrong or dated; follow the most trustworthy source you have, and use your own judgement.

How to open what's linked here: PDFs and .html guides open in any web browser (just click). .zim archives open in Kiwix — open the Kiwix reader, then search inside the archive (see how to use Kiwix). If a link doesn't open, that part wasn't included in your build.

🩺 Medical emergency

The two fastest references are Where There Is No Doctor (clear, illustrated, written for people with no medical training) and the Army First Aid manual. Open the PDF, then use its bookmarks or search (Ctrl+F) for the words in bold.

Historical herbals (not medical advice): Medical\Natural-Medicine\ holds King's American Dispensatory and Culpeper's Complete Herbal as historical references on medicinal plants. They are old (1600s–1900s), often inaccurate, and some remedies are unsafe. Use current sources (WikiMed, Where There Is No Doctor) for actual care — see the medical read-me.

Make water safe to drinkEPA Emergency Disinfection of Drinking Water · Where There Is No Doctor (search "water")When in doubt, boil it: a rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above ~2,000 m / 6,500 ft) kills germs. Use the EPA guide for filtering and chemical treatment.
